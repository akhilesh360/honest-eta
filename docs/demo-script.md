# Honest ETA: 60-second walkthrough script

**0:00–0:08 · Hook.** "A late order hurts trust more than a long estimate. So I tested what happens if Uber Eats promised a range instead of one number."

**0:08–0:18 · Problem.** "Today the app says '15 minutes.' On real New York taxi trips, which I used as a proxy for delivery legs, a one-number promise was late 57% of the time."

**0:18–0:38 · Demo.** "Here's the prototype. I drag distance and pickup hour, and the ETA updates from real trip percentiles. Left: one number. Right: an honest range, 10 to 20 minutes. Switch to P20–P80 and it's tighter, but late more often. Now I simulate a delay: the app says why, and gives a new range."

**0:38–0:50 · Result.** "On 17,600 held-out trips, a P10–P90 range cut late arrivals from 57% to 14%, for about 8½ minutes of range width."

**0:50–0:60 · Next.** "The risk is conversion: a longer quote may scare people off. So I'd A/B test on repeat users first, with checkout conversion as the guardrail."
